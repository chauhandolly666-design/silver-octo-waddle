Question 1

Computer Characteristics & Generations

Explore the evolutionary timeline of computing technology from bulky vacuum tubes to ultra-fast quantum processors, highlighting core operational characteristics.

Speed

Capable of executing millions to billions of instructions per second with zero fatigue.

Accuracy

High precision computing following programmed algorithms strictly (Garbage In, Garbage Out).

Storage

Massive capacity to store data and retrieve instantly via primary and secondary hierarchies.

Versatility

Adaptable to diverse tasks ranging from data processing to complex simulations.

5 Generations of Computers

1st Gen (1940-1956)

Vacuum Tubes

ENIAC, UNIVAC. Massive size, high heat emission, machine language only.

2nd Gen (1956-1963)

Transistors

Smaller, faster, reliable. Assembly and early high-level languages (FORTRAN).

3rd Gen (1964-1971)

Integrated Circuits

Silicon chips, keyboards, monitors, operating systems introduced.

4th Gen (1971-Present)

Microprocessors

VLSI technology, personal computers, internet boom, mobile computing.

5th Gen (Present & Beyond)

Artificial Intelligence

Parallel processing, ULSI, neural networks, quantum computing integration.

Block Diagram & Computer Architecture

Deconstruction of the von Neumann architecture showing the seamless interaction between Input, Output, Storage, Control Unit (CU), and Arithmetic Logic Unit (ALU).

Input Unit

Accepts raw data and instructions from external users and converts them into machine-readable binary signals.

Keyboard, Mouse, Scanner, Sensor
Central Processing Unit (CPU)

The core brain containing the ALU (calculations) and CU (instruction decoding & timing control).

ALU
Control Unit
Output Unit

Translates processed binary data back into human-understandable visual, auditory, or physical formats.

Monitor, Printer, Projector, Speaker

Evaluation of physical access control mechanisms, RFID/NFC smart cards, biometric badge scanners, and campus security gate architectures.

Speed: Low | Security: Ultra High
